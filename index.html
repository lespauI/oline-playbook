Mike: the declared protection reference, usually the middle linebacker or the likely blitzer. These pass examples place Mike toward the called slide. Confirm the call; do not change direction silently.

Center in pass protection: an NT on C is the first job. With no NT, C is the middle blitz picker. Set backward and stay square. The center uses a shorter inside set. Tackles kick back and out to form the pocket. Set depth depends on the defender and the pass timing; keep the interior depth coordinated. The gray O-line markers show the start, and the solid markers show the set position. A 3–4 normally has a nose. The 4–3 example here uses two 3-technique tackles and no nose; a different 4–3 alignment may put a nose back on C.

90 / 91 full-slide: 90 goes right; 91 goes left. Set backward and protect the called gaps. The backside tackle reads his nearest down rusher. Head-up or outside: call BOB, lock him, and keep the inside secure. Inside the tackle: join the slide. Four linemen slide when he uses BOB; all five slide when he joins. BOB means big on big, not chasing a stunt across the formation. HB checks inside first and takes the next backside edge threat. Call and secure each handoff before releasing a rusher.

Run combos: ACE = center + guard; DEUCE = guard + tackle. Both secure the down lineman first. The helper climbs only when the partner controls him. 20 goes directly through center; 21 hits the left A-gap between C and LG; 22 hits the right A-gap between C and RG. On 20, the first step drives straight ahead. On 21, it zones left; on 22, it zones right. Small green arrows show these steps; the flat-ended lines show the example block assignments. 23 aims between LG and LT; 24 aims between RG and RT, and 25/26 stretch wider to the edge.

Stretch pull rule: An NT takes priority: the backside guard pulls (RG on 25 left; LG on 26 right), and C stays on N. Against a 3-tech, C and the backside guard communicate and call one puller; the other player takes the 3-tech. The buttons show either example. Against a 5-tech with no NT or 3-tech inside, C pulls. A 5-tech end does not override an NT or 3-tech inside. Inside blockers secure the pulling player’s gap before he leaves. If an inside threat remains free, he stays and calls CHECK. The puller leads outside only after that coverage is secure.

H = edge help: an additional TE or H-back block is shown in the full 3–4 and 4–3 run looks. Assign the remaining formation blocks before using the play. A dangerous backside edge needs a declared answer; use the called CAP or SPLIT answer. Extra simultaneous blitzers require a protection adjustment or a QB hot answer.

Before the snap: Red-ring defenders on run pages still need a formation block, read, or check. A flow read is conditional: a linebacker who fills inside must have an answer before the pull. A lone edge pass blitz can use the called EDGE exchange when Mike drops and the inside is clear. On 60/61, edge plus Mike creates a local four-on-three overload; protect inside first and call the named hot route or change the protection.
